    How NetBox fields map to the CMDB
    Identity & hardware

    Name, serial, manufacturer/model, management IP, site/location and rack placement become structured CMDB fields.

    Classification

    Recognized role terms map to controlled CI classes. Custom NetBox roles stay visible as attributes and use the neutral Device class.

    Status & lifecycle

    Operational status and lifecycle are mapped separately. Offline is Down, not Retired. Planned and staged records are Planned.

    Virtualization & IPAM

    VM platform, host, cluster, type, sizing, virtual disks, interfaces, primary and assigned IP details are kept as attributes.

    Always refreshed: identity/hardware fields, status, lifecycle, recognized environment custom field, and rack placement. Values removed in NetBox are cleared in ServiceOps.

    Preserved as NetBox attributes: tenant hierarchy, ownership, functional role, platform, raw status, descriptions, timestamps, coordinates, configuration context, tags, custom fields, all assigned IPs/DNS/VRF context, interfaces/VLANs/cables, console, power and cooling connectivity, modules and bays, front/rear ports, inventory items, VM interfaces and virtual disks. Rack metadata is retained on the rack. Endpoints denied to the read-only token are reported as warnings.

    Never inferred: NetBox tenant is not an owning support team, role is not accepted verbatim as a CI class, and cluster is not a physical location. Operational ownership and business data remain ServiceOps-controlled.

    Matching: typed NetBox object ID, then serial number for physical devices, then exact name. Imported elevations appear under Racks.

    How Snipe-IT fields map to the CMDB
    Identity & hardware

    Asset name (or asset tag), serial, manufacturer, model and current location become structured CMDB fields. An IP address custom field fills the CI's IP.

    Classification

    Recognized category terms (laptop, desktop, server, monitor, phone, printer, network gear) map to CI classes. Other categories use the neutral Hardware Asset class.

    Status & lifecycle

    Deployed is In Use, ready to deploy and pending are Planned, undeployable is Down, archived is Retired.

    Assignment & purchase

    The person an asset is checked out to becomes the CI owner when a ServiceOps user has the same email. Purchase and warranty dates fill the CI dates.

    Always refreshed on Snipe-IT items: name, serial, manufacturer, model, location, purchase date, warranty expiry, status, lifecycle, class and owner. A returned (checked-in) asset loses its owner.

    Items NetBox already manages: NetBox stays the source of truth for hardware and status. Snipe-IT only adds purchase and warranty dates, the owner and its own attributes.

    Preserved as Snipe-IT attributes: asset ID and tag, a link to the Snipe-IT record, category, status label, company, supplier, order number, purchase cost, book value, warranty, end of life, default location, assignee, checkout and audit dates, notes and every custom field.

    Matching: Snipe-IT asset ID, then serial number, then exact name. Two Snipe-IT assets with the same name stay separate items; the second gets its asset tag added to its name.

    Not imported: licenses, accessories, consumables and components. Assets deleted in Snipe-IT are not removed from the CMDB.

    Recognized spreadsheet columns

    Upload a CSV, paste CSV text, or point at a published Google Sheet (File → Share → Publish to web, or a link shared as "Anyone with the link can view").

    Recognized columns: Host/Name, Owner/System Owner (resolved to an owning support team, not an individual), Description, Environment, Serial Number, Vendor, Model, Location, Cost Center, Business Criticality, Lifecycle State, Install/Shipped Date, End of Support/Warranty Expiry, IP Address, State/Status (decommissioned rows are detected automatically).

    Other columns (CPUs, RAM, Builder, iDRAC, DNS, …) are kept as-is on the CI's additional imported fields. Fields on CIs synced from NetBox or Snipe-IT that the sync already owns are left untouched.
